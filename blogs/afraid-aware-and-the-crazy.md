# Afraid, Aware and the Crazy

*Published: 2023-05-21*

---

I only have a faint idea of what I want to write today. I think I'm going to swing a Michael today.  
Each one of us has a comfort movie. Mine is to watch Dev from Tamasha. Especially the part where Tara finds him at a Social(the CP one?) and tries to wake him up from a what is according to her a limbo he's been living in. It's fascinating to see how hard she pushes. Him and herself too. Even though the movie suggests that Ved goes through the transformation, I feel it's equally the same for Tara. I would never imagine the bubbly girl from Corsica going to that extent for a guy she met for only few days. She seemed more "sensible" in the conventional sense. And later, she pushes Ved to a breaking point and has hurt herself in the process throughout too. Ved looks at her with reproach (after the whole engagement ring fiasco), then through anger and goes haywire (outside Tara's apartment up the stairs),  bargains for acceptance of his strange behaviour and then quits his job, goes into depression. He completes the whole cycle of grief before he finally accepts who he really is. DABDA - Denial, Anger, Bargain, Depression and Acceptance. And I think what he is grieving over here, is the death of the identity he created for himself while growing up. The identity that Tara nudges into destruction and the whole edifice collapses for Ved.  
If you think about it, Tamasha's narrative also fits "The 3 acts of a Magic Trick" style. The pledge, where you introduce something ordinary to the audience - an archetypal couple romancing in Corsica. The turn, where you make that something ordinary disappear - Ved becoming a totally different person and the old Ved disappears. And then the prestige, where the magician brings that object back - the old Ved comes back to life. Tara, being the agent of the chaos here. The magician. In the scene we see Ved paying her obeisance to the magician by doing a dandvat pranam. Or maybe the magician here instead is the childhood or "bachpan" as the autowala refers in the movie. Isn't the reflection on his childhood is what brings the old Ved back? The magic of memories. Is this what people always keep talking about?  
But this has been a long digression. I warned you before, this is an unplanned entry. I came here to talk about how the two of my favourite Imtiaz Ali creations - Tamasha and Rockstar, depict the fundamental three kinds of people in my opinion. The old Ved, the new Ved and Jordan urf JJ.  
The old Ved lives out of fear. He lives to please the society, follows all the dogma and is afraid to checkin with his emotions to face the reality. Unfailingly he runs away from what he could be, only to serve his fear.  The new Ved is self-aware. He connects with the inner beauty and nonchalantly breaks off the expectations the society(read father) had set for him. He no longer lives out of fear. It becomes easy for him to apologise to Tara and comes back to theatre, where he always felt home. In fact the new Ved is the same Ved we meet as a child. We even see glimpses of that Ved trying to come out in Corsica when he gathers a whole Church party at dinner and narrates them a story as a big Indian director. And Jordan from Rockstar, he is the crazy kind. These are folks who are neither self-aware but nor they live out of any fear. The dangerous kind. JJ has no fucks to give when he is thrown out from his family, rejected by Heer, fucked over by Dhingra, beaten up by police or wooed by Sheena, the press girl. He is absolutely wild as nature, as he claims in the Sadda Haq song. You can almost draw a parallel between Ved and Aditya. Both grow out of a society pleasing puppet to be liberated to live the full life. Personally, I have not seen many of the crazy kind, but whenever  I have, they come off as too eccentric for my liking and I think I've always maintained my distance. But understanding the first two ways in how we operate gave me more empathy towards others and myself. You never know, when life can give you a chance to be Tara for a Ved and complete you both. Or when it can present you with a magician in a form of a person or a situation, to liberate you from that cycle of fear and come to find the real you.
