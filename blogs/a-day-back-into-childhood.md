# A day back into childhood

*Published: 2023-02-04*

---

Layoffs are around. There's panic everywhere. My friend and I bunked the office and spent the day out. "Thriving in ambiguity" is a core principal at work. It was one of the best days in recent past, here's how it went.  
We started the day early and left our place at around 8:30 am. I packed a bag full of snacks, an apple, protein bars, some nuts and a gallon of water bottle. My mother taught me well I guess. The morning looked extra fresh today. Maybe because there was a sense of freedom in doing something other than the crowd. When the masses were rushing to their offices, we were in our half pants driving towards the local zoo. On reaching the zoo, we found that it might have not been the best to come on a weekday. It was deserted, there was no one but us. We took out tickets and my friend, whom I'll call Morrie here, grabbed a bag of cheetos. There wasnt much for the best 100 yards or so. But then it began. We found a whole section dedicated towards all kinds of monkeys and apes. There were Langurs, Bonobo, Macaque, lion tailed monkey and some others. Since we were alone, we let the inner child out and played silly tricks with them. But the next section blew us away. We were looking at some little cats like leopard  cat, meerkats and few which went in hiding inside the shelter. As I was moving my eyes to the next cage, I saw there was no cage, it was big wide open area with a white tiger walking towards me in all its beauty. I froze for a hot second. Our minds are trained so well to think wild animals only belong in cage, that when you see them open you freeze. Morrie broke the spell on me and I realized then that there was a huge ditch between the open area the tigers were in and the pavement we were walking on. There were 4 white tigers playing in the open grass. The view was breathtaking. I feel there is something so overwhelming to connect with those parts of nature that we have seen only in televisions. Probably the same happens when you travel to exotic places you've only seen in tellies. Anyway next up were Jaguars and Cheetahs. It was mesmerising to sit so close to them. A thin wall of steel mesh separated us. But there I was sitting to an animal which would devour and gorge me within seconds if left open. Is man really the most powerful animal? Walking and eating cheetos we were clicking pictures when we heard a loud roar, it left us no doubt what was coming next. The asiatic lion was a bit disappointing other than its roar. If it were upto me, I would award King of the jungle to The Royal Bengal Tiger anyday. Lions dont appeal me so much. I dont know, maybe I miss the beauty in them. Then there was Rhino, Hyena, Jackal, Ostrich, Deer, Hippo, Girrafee, Aligators, Crocs and a bird park. But I was waiting for the best. The OGs of the parsel-tongue. I have had so many dreams about snakes throughout my life that I am sure a snake would be the cause of my death. I would be brushing my teeth peacefully in morning when out of the blue an intrusive thought would hit me that there would be a snake crawling on the floor right behind me. Or it might be sitting inside the cupboard under the sink. Despite of the fear, there is an obsession. As we entered the building which had glass chambers for the snakes, we saw a large swarm of little school kids jumping around the barricades. What a co-incidence. As I looked at the Reticulated Python, I couldnt help myself from wishing that the glass breaks and someone gets invited to Hogwarts. It didnt but the experience was nonetheless magical. As we walked out of the zoo, we felt as if we had taken a trip back in time.  
Next up was to go the fair but it was still sunny so we decided to kill time in a theatre. Pathan was playing and we went in with zero expectations and came out happier than we assumed. YRF multiverse is an interesting concept and I especially love cross-overs.  
Blasting off the music from Pathan we drove to the fair which unfortunately had no parking and here began our series of bribes for the day. First one went to the watchmen of a government building who let us in for fifty bucks. We went in through a street of hawkers and entered the Numaish exhibition and there it stood in its grandiosity and hosting more than 10k people at once. There was something for everyone. Local craftsmen, vendors selling Agra Petha, Chaat, Samosas, Ber, toys, cutlery, popcorn, laser lights, stuffed animals and what not. There were 50+ stalls that we saw for Kashmiri Dry fruits. There were cultural dance performances happening and a band performance alongside. It was huge. I purchased few ceramics, some gizmos for back massage, attars and Bakhoor. We bribed a shopkeeper while his owner was away and Morrie and I played an arm strength contest which I lost. To try my luck again I played the usual games at a funfair and I lost there too. The soft toys were appealing enough to shell money on them blindly. On the bright side, I earned some good karma by helping a lost kid get to his mom. The highlight was of-course the Well of Death. This was nostalgia in full throttle. Cars and bikes running a couple of feet away from our face with no gravity brought back all the memories from the childhood. We bribed the local guard and stayed on the well for a couple of more rounds. It was an exhilarating experience overall and the child in me is satisfied until the next time!  
PS: I wrote this one to try my hand at writing about simple things and events and not necessarily about abstract ideas. I realised while my vocab about ideas has developed from all the reading and watching debates and participating in discussions, my lexicon for the simple things still comes from the 12th standard english. People who write newsletters or magazine articles might be good at this. More power to them!
