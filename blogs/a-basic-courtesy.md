# A basic courtesy

*Published: 2023-01-29*

---

On my way back from the grocery store, as I waited for my apartment elevator I noticed an old lady probably in her 80s. Dressed in blue floral saree she was carrying a small packet of flowers, probably back from her evening visit to the society temple. She entered the lift before me and then said "Come" to the air. After a hot second I realised there was no kid around, the remark was meant for me. She was speaking to me. The elevator had just climbed 2 floors and she got off. "Okay, see you again". I had only been processing the self assuring personality of hers but the sheer comfort with which she said those words shook me even more. I was overwhelmed with positive emotion and managed to reply, "You have a good evening". I was completely soaked in her aura. Usually I get little too careful around old people, helping them press the elevator button etc. but this woman was very self reliant. But I digress. What I wanted to pen down was something that I've felt odd since forever.  

Smiling at strangers. Most of us walk around like we carry the weight of the world on our shoulders. Always preoccupied with something. Lost in thought we manoeuvre around the people on the street or in the hallway avoiding as much eye contact as possible. Don't get me wrong, Im not talking about foolishly smiling at everyone that passes by you. But I feel we certainly do a bad job in acknowledging the existence of fellow humans. How many times has it happened to you that you see someone attractive from a distance away and you checkout their attire, hairstyle or footwear whatever appeals to your fantasy? And just when the person gets closer to you, you look dead straight into the air as if reporting news to a camera up front. Why do we do that? Why not simply accept that, smile or just nod to that person and maybe drop a soft compliment? Why does it have to be creepy? Doesn't everyone admire the beauty in others? I believe once we start being open about our feelings, we start appreciating ourselves more and maybe even put in more efforts to be better?  

I think what this also forces us to do, is to keep our emotions resolved. Be free of thought. It circles back to being in the moment. I think it also builds that charismatic quality of being comfortable in the space around you. You watch some people walking around at the office, at a decent pace, not running around, comfortable to walk past you without an air of creep or discomfort. Doesn't that tell a lot about their headspace?
