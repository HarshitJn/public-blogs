# If I were a kid.

*Published: 2023-01-21*

---

"Why is there fear in sickness?", Morrie put that question to my face almost stopping me as we started to walk towards the canteen."Just the thought that I could be doing so much more instead of being a lump of sadness coiled inside a blanket". I don't know what gave it away. It was like he could smell my fears or see when something was eating me up. I had been sick the previous week and the discontent probably showed up on my face. "Doesn't answer the question still", Morrie said politely.
I knew Morrie, who was I kidding. He wants me to dig the deepest of my hearts and then respond. "I think it stems from lot of childhood trauma since I used to be sick all the time.", I said casually. I had shared deeper fears of mine with him. Big whoop. "I had a chronic case of asthma until my teens and fevers of 104 would knock my doors at least once a quarter. Every season change. Ive seen my mom stay up the nights putting cold towel to my forehead to reduce fever. Couple of episodes of Typhoid and Chikungunya broke my will"."That explains a lot". Morrie said. I felt lighter. I don't know why. Maybe because it reassured that the burden that my recent illness was works of fate or just bad genes. If you know me, you know I can be be too harsh on myself sometimes. We talked some more about how Morrie dealt with sickness but he cut that conversation short and said, "Enough of this shit talk, let's talk about something else". I knew something interesting was coming up. Morrie and I would independently wonder about life and discuss those for hours in our weekly lunches.
"Can adults learn to live like children?", he blurted out."I think so. Yeah. Why not. We conditioned ourselves to be adults. We should be able to decondition ourselves.", was my first thought but deeper I knew better."Whats the main challenge you think?", he asked as he ate his piping hot rasam."We fear a lot. We have bunch of goals and aspirations. We have strong opinions of rights and wrongs and most importantly we wont let go". I had thought about this for a long time. This was the distilled answer from a year long fiddling with the self.
He picked it up from miles away and said, "But why can we not have both"."Both?", I was lost."Why cant we be both pragmatic or logical in our approach when we face tough times but also be totally detached from the moment as if we don't care.", Morrie said moving onto his next dish while I was still shuffling the cowpeas in my salad."You mean be a peaceful warrior?" I plugged my favourite movie in my response."Yeah. Have your eyes on the prize and still not care if you win or lose", he added."Well I would want to do that but I think the problem comes because we cant process two emotions at the same time. How can my brain both be angry or driven to do something big and at the same time be totally calm about it. I just don't think we are wired that way", I responded after ruminating over that thought."But our brains can multitask. I can cycle a bike while listening to a podcast", he continued."Well maybe cognitive brain isn't the same as emotional brain. I cant process if I could be sad and happy at the same time.", I was lost in my head."What about when you hear the Requiem for a Dream", he blurted out almost instantly. I even forgot we had discussed about that movie."Maybe when you listen to that song, it's not that you're feeling both the emotions simultaneously but that you feel a different emotion which has hints of them both.", I gushed and was amused myself at this thought. I felt as if I invented a new emotion."What if you get to that state in my analogy. A new conscious experience where you are always detached, at peace and over that you feel all sorts of emotions like anger or fear", Morrie maintained."But wouldn't it create a split brain problem. One part of the brain taking a supreme Buddha role and dominating the other part to feel controlled anger etc. I doubt that would work well. You might live in a perennial state of self conflict.""Let me remind you Im not talking about two levels but two parallels", Morrie said with an earnest face."I think having any kind of an internal split is a doorway to hell. I tried that once and I created more self conflict than I solved outside. What works for me is the interrupt model where I let myself flow with whatever emotion the situation demands and almost naturally an interrupt comes which tells me to detach and let that go." I talked about this in the last blog post."That sounds good. Just reverse that. Let yourself flow in peace and let the other emotions come as interrupt, resolve themselves and disappear.", Morrie weighed in."And I think these interrupts get faster and easier with practice. When I started meditating 4 years back, I used to push myself to be in that state for 10 mins of meditative practice and now I can stay like that for hours. Maybe if continue to practice, meditative state becomes our first nature." I found myself agreeing to Morrie's point.
We rambled some more, the details of which I will spare.  More than an hour went by. We forgot how we went from softly talking while eating to debating loudly in the canteen to walking down in the sun. We were fully absorbed. We hardly saw who walked past us or if people were noticing these 2 nerds walking in the sun when everyone was taking the path under shade. We were 100% in the moment. We were kids. For that time. In that time.
PS: This was a rather tough entry to write. I have a new found respect for novelists. It's rather difficult to give voices to characters inside your head. To provide the context and convey emotions in dialogue without explicitly mentioning them. And I think I picked a very abstract topic to my own detriment. Nevertheless it was amazingly fun to write and recollect my thoughts. Until next time.
Japaan me baj gaye hain baara, sayonaara..
